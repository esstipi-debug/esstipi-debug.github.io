# esstipi-debug.github.io

Short field notes by Marcos Stipicich: one question per page, each with one mechanism and one source.

The site is generated. Edit `src/pages/*.md`, then run:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python build.py
python -m pytest
```

`build.py` writes the HTML, assets, `files/` and `sitemap.xml` inside `landpage/`, and writes `robots.txt`, a neutral `404.html` and `.nojekyll` at the repository root (so `/` serves 404). Folders of pages that were removed from `src/pages/` are deleted on the next build. Files in `src/files/` (the one-page plan, the two fonts, the pictures of the desk and the covers of the field notes) are copied to `landpage/files/`.

## Page types

| `type` | What it is | Keys beyond the required ones |
|---|---|---|
| `home` | The home page: the headline (with an optional desk picture under it) beside the sheet where a situation is picked, the doors by kind of reader and by floor, four figures each with its source, the method, the argument, then the field notes | `headline`, `lede` (twenty words at most), repeated `symptom`, optional `kicker` (the role: one line under the two actions of the hero, never above the headline), `base_line`, `secondary: label \| slug`, `subject` |
| `landing` | An entry page for one situation, one floor or one kind of buyer, shown as a door on the home page | `group: situation`, `floor` or `buyer`, `door`, `door_text`, `kicker`, `subject` |
| `page` | Engagements, how I work, proof, working terms, contact | optional `headline`, `kicker`, `subject`, `cta`, repeated `offer: name \| amount in USD \| unit` |
| `note` | A set of field notes: its page, with the way to its PDF | `kicker`, `pdf`, `pdf_pages`, and the two lines its entry opens with on the author page: `about` (what the note is about) and `takeaway` (what the reader takes away). Both are required, and together they are the note's `description` cut in two: the same words in the same order, without the label that names the reader |
| `question`, `author`, `book` | The short answers, the author page and the manuscript | see the existing pages |

Every landing page carries the same four sections: *What I check first*, *Where I have stood*, *What I don't claim* and *How we would start*. Every commercial page carries one contact button, an email whose subject line is the page's `subject`. It closes the page, unless the page marks a place for it under its headline with `<!-- contact -->` on a line of its own (the proof page does): then the page closes with the same call as a link.

The home page asks first what is happening, not who the visitor is: beside the headline it names the symptoms (`symptom` in the front matter, one sentence each) and asks which one is the reader's. Three radio buttons pick it, with no script, and each situation then shows its drawing, the figure that measures it and its door. The doors by kind of buyer and by floor come right after, with the door for whoever is hiring and who the work fits best, both read from the pages that say them and never retyped. A situation page adds a fifth section, *What each of you will ask first*, with one question each from finance, operations and the chief executive, so the page can be forwarded.

`python desk.py <picture>` cuts the desk picture to 16:9, fades its edges into the page and writes it twice to `src/files/desk/` (`desk.webp` and `desk-800.webp`); from the `details` marks in `src/desk.json` (left, top, width and height in percent), it also cuts `yard.webp`, the background of the *How I work* pillar in the earlier design (the present one does not draw it: the file and the `has-yard` class stay for when pictures return). The two slots gate independently (`hero_desk_ready()` and `detail_ready("yard")` in `build.py`), and `--only hero` or `--only details` writes one without requiring the other. Without `desk.webp` and `desk-800.webp`, the hero cleanly renders as single-column copy.

The method section of the home page is three steps, *Measure*, *Cut* and *Stitch* (`METHOD_STEPS` in `build.py`), each beside the same line drawn in a new state (`drawings.py`): line work only, no photograph and no picture of a tool. `plates.py` still turns generated scenes into two-colour plates (`python plates.py <folder>`, from images named `escena-1.png` to `escena-4.png`), but the home page does not show them at present. Pillow is needed only by `plates.py`, `desk.py`, the covers of `shelf.py` and their tests.

`build.py` holds the pages, the data and the helpers; `home.py` lays the home page out and `drawings.py` holds its line drawings (the record as a white line, the floor as a red one; a drawing that is not to scale says so). `risks.py` draws the two evidence plates from `src/risks.json`: on the proof page, two lines for each risk on one scale (how far I went, and how far a stranger can check it); on the engagements page, a mark for each coded job posting and for my engagement, with the count the sentence over it states. `page_parts.py` answers what an inner page asks for in its own text: `<!-- contact -->`, `<!-- drawing: claims -->` (the drawing of the audit's claims, the same one the home page shows) and a list inside `<div class="rows">`, which becomes rows that fold on a phone and stand open on a wide screen. The proof and engagements pages are rows and lines, with no boxes: the engagements are a rate sheet that opens one at a time on a phone and stands open on a wide screen, and what happens after the work is two spans drawn on one scale. `shelf.py` lays the field notes out on the author page, as a shelf: a title alone does not say what a note is about, so each entry opens with it (`about`), says what the reader takes away (`takeaway`) and then gives the note's name and its file. The entries stand under the reader the note is written for (the `for the CEO`, `CFO` or `COO` of its description; a note that names no reader stands last, under its `kicker`) and a filter leaves one reader, with radio buttons and no script. On a phone the list opens one note at a time and a shut entry still says what the note is about; from 64rem up every entry stands open. Nothing is printed twice: the line that says what a note is about is also the label of its radio button. Each entry shows the cover of its file, the first page of the PDF, kept small in `src/files/covers/` and made by `python shelf.py <folder>`, where the folder holds each PDF as `<slug>.pdf` (it needs PyMuPDF and Pillow, like `python book.py`, and stops if a PDF does not have the pages its note states). A note without a cover prints without one; the tests say whether every note has its own. The home page shows the same shelf in its field notes section (`library` in `home.py` calls `shelf.shelf`), so the notes have one layout on both pages; the links of an entry carry their own underline, so that the shelf looks the same inside the column of the author page and outside it. `src/style.css` is the whole site and `src/home.css` only the home page. Both are dark only, with one accent and no rounded corners; what moves does so with CSS and stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Every colour is a token in `:root` of `style.css` and is used by name. Labels (table heads, captions, the line over a card) are the body face in sentence case, without letter-spacing; the mono face is kept for figures and prices. No link carries an arrow, no line joins three items with middle dots, no page prints a dash, and nothing small stands above a headline: on the inner pages the line that names the section sits under the headline, in one `<hgroup>`.

Both style sheets open with the stamp of the linter that checked them, `sloplint` from the Hallmark skill. It reads the built files, so build first:

```bash
python build.py
node <hallmark>/scripts/sloplint.mjs landpage/index.html landpage/style.css landpage/home.css
```

The stamp says what was checked (all 36 pages of `landpage/` and the two style sheets), what was set aside and why. To check everything at once:

```bash
node <hallmark>/scripts/sloplint.mjs landpage
```

The check of 2026-11-19, after the redesign of the home, proof and engagements pages, ran the static tier on every page and on the two style sheets. The render tier ran the same day on those three pages only (`HALLMARK_PUPPETEER` set to a folder that holds `puppeteer-core`, `CHROME_PATH` set to Chrome, and `--url` pointed at the local preview). It no longer fails anything: the contact button of the proof page now stands under its headline (gate 44b) and the radar button of the engagements page no longer wraps at 320 (gate 49a). What it still asks for stays as it is, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-19. It is the linter's rule for a landing page set against pages of rows: a headline that fills the first screen (44c and 79, on the three pages; on the two inner ones it takes the site header for the hero, because their headline stands in no `header` or `section`), section heads about twice as large (78), 120px between sections (59) and wider or heavier sections (77). On the engagements page it also notes that a phone folds text a wide screen shows (F25): that is how the rate sheet is meant to work. The author page was checked with the render tier the same day, after the shelf of field notes went in: the shelf adds one note of the same kind (F25, the entries a phone keeps shut), and what that page was already asked for stays as it was (a contact button inside the first screen, 44b, and a narrower column, 25). The home page shows the same shelf and gets the same F25 and nothing else new. Before the redesign, with `--render`, the reading pages still asked for the contact button inside the first screen and for a narrower column (gates 44b and 25). With `--render` the linter writes screenshots to a `.hallmark/` folder next to the page: that folder is ignored by git and must stay out of `landpage/`. The two typefaces, IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono (both under the SIL Open Font License), are in `src/files/fonts/`: no page asks another host for a font. The folder also keeps the two files of the earlier design, Archivo and JetBrains Mono, which the radar page (`atlas/`) still loads by itself.

`tests/test_proof_engagements.py` holds the rules of the proof and engagements pages as working drawings (figures that jump to their section, rows that fold, the claims drawn as on the home page, the rate sheet, the two spans after the work) and `tests/test_risks.py` those of the two plates. `tests/test_shelf.py` holds the rules of the shelf of field notes on the author page: one entry per note and no summary printed twice, the two lines of a note made of the words of its description, the groups by reader and the filter (whose only rules that hide a group name the pick of another reader), the covers, the list that folds on a phone and stands open on a wide screen, and the home page, which shows the same shelf and keeps nothing of the table it had before. `tests/test_tap_targets.py` keeps what a finger has to hit at 44px where the letters are small: a list of links (`class="links"` on the list or on a `div` around it), a link that is most of a small line (a source, an origin, a byline), the page pills of the book reader, which share one row on a phone, and the filter, the lines and the links of the shelf. `tests/test_commercial.py` holds the rules for the commercial pages: prices only on the engagements page and only in USD, the employer named once and only on the proof page, no banned words, one voice (the site speaks as "I", never as a firm's "we"), the *Who to hire for what* table on the engagements page, the home page from top to bottom (a hero that fits a first screen, four figures each with its source and its page, independent hero/yard gating and preserved floor boundaries, no maker named), no script on any page, fonts served by the site itself, the look (every colour a token, no label in capitals or with spread letters, no arrow in a link, one email address, every file a style sheet points to exists), and `FIGURES`, the list of every number a commercial page may print with its source. A new number fails the tests until it is added there.
