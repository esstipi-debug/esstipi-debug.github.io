Put the Toll on the Bridge

Field notes for the COO

By Marcos Stipicich · 16 November 2026

A step that guards an expensive mistake earns its place on a shift screen. The same step on every line teaches the crew to get around it.

In 1919 the civil engineer Frederic Wood wrote the history of New England’s toll roads. Where the ground was open, travellers often took a short road around the gate, a shunpike; where a marsh flanked the gate, they stayed on the road. In a lab study by Zana Buçinca, Maja Barbara Malaya and Krzysztof Gajos, the designs meant to make people think before an AI answered caught more of its errors, and the most effective got the least favourable ratings. My reading: on the floor the coin is seconds per transaction, and a step placed where a mistake is expensive and hard to undo buys attention; placed on every line, it buys a shunpike.

The field notes end with four questions for the next screen you approve, and the arithmetic to start with: list every step that is not the work, time it on the floor at the busiest hour, and ask which of those minutes ever caught a mistake.
