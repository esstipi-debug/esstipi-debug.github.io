Use It or Lose It

Field notes for the CEO

By Marcos Stipicich · 16 November 2026

When a budget expires on a fixed date, the rule decides who pays for the rush.

The economists Jeffrey Liebman and Neale Mahoney looked at the dates of US federal purchases from 2004 to 2009. Spending spiked in the last week of the fiscal year, and the IT projects started that week were rated worse. At the Department of Justice, which could carry part of its IT money into later years, the IT rush nearly disappeared. My reading: the person rushing is not necessarily the one who pays. The bill goes to whoever inherits what was bought.

The field notes end with the step for a CEO: find the rules that pay for a rush, such as a quarter-end target or a project budget that ends at go-live, and who carries what each causes. Then change what the date does to the money.
