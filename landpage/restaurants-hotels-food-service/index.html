Industry

Kitchens, hotels and food service

Inventory and purchasing software goes into a kitchen that runs on a prep list, a walk-in and the chef’s memory. The count happens after midnight, the variance shows up a week later, and nobody can say whether it was waste, portioning, theft or a receiving error.

What I check first

Receiving: are weight, temperature and date captured at the door, or rebuilt later from the invoice? Prep: is trim loss recorded per batch, so that food cost is a measurement and not an estimate? The walk-in and the cellar: is first-expired-first-out physically possible with the shelves as they are? The screen on the line: is it faster than shouting?

Where I have stood

A kitchen is just-in-time manufacturing with a clock on the wall. I bring thirteen years of planning, inventory and distribution to it, and I have worked in professional kitchens in Chile and Australia, from fine dining to quick service.

What I don’t claim

I have not run a restaurant group or a hotel food-and-beverage department. I work on the supply chain underneath them.

How we would start

Four weeks of receiving, count and sales exports from two of your sites.
