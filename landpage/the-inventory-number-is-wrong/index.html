Stock and cash

The inventory number is wrong

The balance sheet carries the inventory at one number. The floor holds another, and the gap is cash.

What I check first

Record accuracy, by blind count. Stock that has not moved and still sits on the books. Shrink, spoilage and damage: whether they are written off when they happen or at period end. Orders shipped wrong, and what each one costs to put right.

Where I have stood

As the analyst on a USD 100M inventory position, then as operations manager on a USD 30M P&L, where about USD 5M of inventory was released. Earlier, about USD 2M a year of savings identified during a warehouse-system implementation.

What I don’t claim

I am not your auditor and I do not sign the accounts. I show you where the number and the floor disagree, and what it is worth.

How we would start

Three weeks, fixed price: your exports, a counted sample, and a written list of losses in the order of what they cost, with the count behind each one.

What each of you will ask first

Finance: “How do I know it pays back?” You know before you spend anything else. The fee is fixed, and by week three you hold a list of losses in money, each with the count behind it. What to do next is your call, with that list in hand.

Operations: “Will counting stop my floor?” No. The count is a sample, taken inside the shifts as they run. No stopped dock and no extra meetings for your supervisors.

The chief executive: “Why can’t my own people do this?” They can count. What is hard from the inside is saying that your own number is wrong. I have no number to defend, and I leave the count method with your team.
