Technology vendors

For teams that deploy AI, robots or a WMS into physical operations

A pilot can pass every acceptance test and still be bypassed on the night shift. Not out of malice: when the dock is full, the old way is faster.

What I check first

Your customer’s record, read the way your system will read it: how much of it is true. What the crew is paid for, and whether your system measures the same thing. Where your screen is slower than the workaround.

Where I have stood

I have lived the record your agent will read, and the people who write it: thirteen years in planning, inventory and distribution, and a 50,000-SKU warehouse-system changeover where I stayed on the floor until the shortcuts died (the record). I learned to code in C, so I read a warehouse the way a C programmer reads memory: every location is an address, a stockout is a pointer to an empty one, and excess stock is memory nobody freed. Today I build with AI coding agents, keeping the math and the rollback in my hands.

What I don’t claim

I have not deployed AI agents for a vendor, and none of the systems I have built runs a client’s operation yet.

How we would start

Before go-live, a records audit of one customer site. During it, floor instruction in the crew’s own terms and a staged go-live with a way back. After it, the 90-day check: who still uses it, who went around it, and why.
