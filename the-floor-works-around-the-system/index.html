After go-live

The system is live and the floor works around it

You paid for the system. The vendor installed it and left. Nobody in the contract was paid to make the floor use it, and the spreadsheet is still beside the terminal.

What I check first

Where people go around the system, shift by shift, and what the workaround saves them. Whether the records the system reads are right. What each shift is paid for. Who owns the system now that the vendor has gone.

Where I have stood

Thirteen years on the operating side: planning for more than 200 stores, a three-site logistics operation of about 100 people, a USD 30M P&L, and a warehouse-system changeover where I stayed on the floor until the old shortcuts died.

What I don’t claim

I do not replace your vendor or your team, and I do not sell a second system. The work is to make the one you already paid for the fastest way to do the job.

How we would start

Three weeks: every shift walked, every workaround listed with what it costs, and the short list of changes that make the system the fast way. Then I stay, part time, until the floor no longer needs the spreadsheet.

What each of you will ask first

The chief executive: “Why hasn’t my own team fixed this?” The people who run the day cannot also stop to redesign it, and the vendor’s contract ended at go-live. It needs one owner with no other job. I take that job, and I hand it back to a named person on your team.

Finance: “We already paid for this system. Why spend more on it?” The first payment bought the software. The return comes when the floor uses it. You get the cost of each workaround in money before you decide anything else.

Operations: “Will you turn my floor upside down?” No. One workaround at a time, starting with the one that costs the most, inside the shifts as they run. Nothing is switched off until the new way is faster than the old one.
