A traceability app, a yield system or a new warehouse system arrives at a shearing shed, a boning room or a parts counter in the middle of the season. The crew has run the job for twenty years on a notebook, a radio and memory. If the new screen is slower than the notebook, the notebook wins.

## What I check first

Where the weight or the count is captured, and how many times it is typed again before it reaches the system. What the crew is paid for, and whether the system measures the same thing. What happens in peak season, on the night shift, or when the connection drops.

## Where I have stood

I grew up on sheep stations in Magallanes, in Chilean Patagonia. From my teens I worked the shearing season, and I learned slaughter and butchery on the station. Later I was outbound leader at a 50,000-SKU agricultural-machinery parts distribution centre in Melbourne, through its warehouse-system changeover ([the record](../proof/#track-record)).

## What I don't claim

I have not managed an abattoir or a cattle operation. I know yield from breaking down a carcass, not from running a plant's P&L.

## How we would start

A records audit from your own exports (receivals, kill sheets, pick logs) before anyone travels.
