Speed is measured the same minute, with a name. The damage it causes is booked weeks later, without one.

Both numbers reach the finance team. The productivity report shows the fastest drivers; the maintenance and shrink accounts show the repairs and the write-offs. In the books I have seen, nobody could subtract one from the other, because nothing connected them. One clock pays in real time, by user code; the other charges later, to nobody. A bonus on the first clock buys damage on the second, and the people earning it are doing what they are paid for.

The field notes end with a way to trace three lines of damage to a shift and a decision. If nobody can, the bonus is paying for them.
