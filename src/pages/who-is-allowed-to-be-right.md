After a go-live, being right about a rule and being allowed to stop it can sit two weeks apart.

In one distribution center I worked in, a new rule sent heavy parts by air for about two weeks. The floor saw it in the boxes and said so; the people who could stop it took the system's word until the invoices arrived. My reading: a wrong rule is ordinary in hypercare, the weeks right after go-live, and catching it is what those weeks are for. The expensive part was the distance between the facts and the right to stop it.

The field notes end with a way to measure that gap for one rule: who sees it fail first, who can stop it, how many days pass in between, and what it costs to say so.
