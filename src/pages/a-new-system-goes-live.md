The new system goes live on a Monday. By Thursday the night shift is back on the spreadsheet, and the orders still have to ship.

## What I check first

Whether the record matches the shelf, by blind count. Where the floor already works around the current system, and why. What each shift is paid for, because people follow the pay before they follow the screen.

## Where I have stood

I closed one warehouse system and opened the next in a 50,000-SKU distribution centre, with a team of 20 and 23 operators trained on RF scanning. I stayed on the floor until the old shortcuts died. Order processing time came down 20%.

## What I don't claim

I do not sell the software and I am not paid by the vendor. I lead the changeover for the operation that has to live with it.

## How we would start

Three weeks: I walk every shift, count a sample, and hand you the list of what will break at go-live, in the order to fix it.

## What each of you will ask first

**Operations: "My supervisors are at their limit. Who carries this?"** I do. The data checks, the floor instruction and the go-live nights are my work, so your supervisors keep shipping.

**Finance: "We already pay the vendor to implement it. Why pay again?"** The vendor is paid to install the system. Nobody is paid to make the floor use it, and that is where the money already spent is won or lost. The fee is fixed and paid by milestone, and the last part is due only when the agreed measures hold 30 days after go-live.

**The chief executive: "What do I get that my vendor and my team don't give me?"** One person whose only job is that the floor ends up using what you bought, and who tells you before go-live what will stop it.
