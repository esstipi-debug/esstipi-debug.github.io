The new system goes live on a Monday. By Thursday the night shift is back on the spreadsheet, and the orders still have to ship.

It is not a small risk. After a US cosmetics maker switched on a new ERP, it could not ship about USD 64 million of sales that year, by its own estimate, and booked USD 53.6 million of extra charges, mostly to restore service to its customers.

Source: [Revlon, Inc., annual report on Form 10-K for 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/887921/000088792119000004/rev201810-k.htm).
{: .src }

## What I check first

Whether the record matches the shelf, by blind count. Where the floor already works around the current system, and why. What each shift is paid for, because people follow the pay before they follow the screen. Whether the extracts from the old system to the new one keep one line per order line, so a one-to-many join does not count open orders twice, and whether the replenishment rules run first in a draft stage, with a way back, before the dock opens.

## Where I have stood

I closed one warehouse system and opened the next in a 50,000-SKU distribution centre, with a team of 20 and 23 operators trained on RF scanning. I stayed on the floor until the old shortcuts died, and order processing time fell about 20% once the parallel spreadsheets and paper tallies were retired.

## What I don't claim

I do not sell the software and I am not paid by the vendor. I lead the changeover for the operation that has to live with it.

## How we would start

Three weeks: I walk every shift, count a sample, and hand you the list of what will break at go-live, in the order to fix it.

## What each of you will ask first

**Operations: "My supervisors are at their limit. Who carries this?"** I do. The data checks, the floor instruction and the go-live nights are my work, so your supervisors keep shipping.

**Finance: "We already pay the vendor to implement it. Why pay again?"** The vendor is paid to install the system. Nobody is paid to make the floor use it, and that is where the money already spent is won or lost. The fee is fixed and paid by milestone, and the last part is due only when the agreed measures hold 30 days after go-live.

**The chief executive: "What do I get that my vendor and my team don't give me?"** One person whose only job is that the floor ends up using what you bought, and who tells you before go-live what will stop it.
